The only thing the view needs is a number. Every change to `value` becomes one native roll: each digit takes the shortest path to its new glyph in the direction of the change, columns that appear slide in from blank, columns that disappear slide out.

A value that arrives while the wheels are still rolling doesn't restart them from rest: the roll continues with the ease-out half of its curve towards the new target, so a live ticker that updates several times a second keeps flowing.

## Timing

`duration` is the length of a roll in milliseconds (`0` snaps). `easing` picks the curve: `linear`, `easeIn`, `easeOut`, `easeInOut` (default) or `spring`, whose overshoot is `bounce` (0–1). `stagger` delays each digit's start by that many milliseconds, least significant digit first, so a change cascades like a mechanical carry.

  code={`<RollingNumber
  value={value}
  groupingSeparator=","
  fontSize={56}
  fontWeight="800"
  easing="spring"   // 'linear' | 'easeIn' | 'easeOut' | 'easeInOut' | 'spring'
  bounce={0.15}     // spring overshoot, 0–1
  duration={500}    // ms
  stagger={30}      // ms between digits, least significant first
/>`}>

</Example>

## The transitions

The roll is not the only way a change can play. `transition` picks one of two others; both swap each changed glyph in place, digits that don't change stay put, and the change cascades from the leftmost changed digit to the right (`stagger`). `duration` and `easing` apply to each, with their own defaults. Under Reduce Motion every one of them snaps, like a roll.

- **`numeric`** is the effect of SwiftUI's `.contentTransition(.numericText())`: the old glyph softens, shrinks and slides out; the new one slides in from the other side, a little small and out of focus, and resolves. The glyphs move up when the value grows and down when it shrinks. The blur is real on iOS, Android and here: a blurred copy of each glyph rendered once per font and cross-faded with the sharp one, so a frame costs what a frame of a roll does.
- **`scramble`**: each changed digit shows a different random digit every few frames, never the one it is leaving or arriving at, and locks on its target from the left.

## The change flash and the pop

Two more things can happen on a change, with any transition. `flashUpColor` and `flashDownColor` are the change flash of a trading screen: every digit whose glyph changes lights up in the up colour when the value grew and the down colour when it shrank, and fades back over `flashDuration` (600 ms). `popOnChange` punches the whole figure on every change, its peak overshoot as a fraction of the size, rung out like the reveal's landing pop; `0.08` is a nudge, `0.2` a slam.

  value={price}
  fractionDigits={2}
  groupingSeparator=","
  prefix="$"
  flashUpColor="#16a34a"
  flashDownColor="#dc2626"
  popOnChange={0.08}
/>`}>

</Example>

## Direction

By default a digit rolls **up** when the number grows and **down** when it shrinks (in the numeric transition, the glyphs move up and down the same way). Force one with `direction="up"` or `"down"`, e.g. for a countdown that should always fall.

```tsx
<RollingNumber value={secondsLeft} direction="down" minimumIntegerDigits={2} />
```
